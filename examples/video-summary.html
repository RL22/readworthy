Rethinking How We Build

Maggie Chen, Structconf 2024 · 42:17 · 84K views · AI-generated summary

TL;DR Timestamps Key Ideas Takeaways
TL;DR — Teams using AI to generate docs produce volume without improving shared understanding. The problem isn't the AI output — it's that specs become write-optimized contracts nobody re-reads. Chen's solution: structure docs for scanning, not reading, and separate Generate / Distill / Operate phases in your writing workflow.

The curator's trap: A spec document is write-optimized by default. The curator adds detail that made sense when writing. The reader needs detail that makes sense for their task. These are not the same document.

Diagnosis 04:20

How specs fail without anyone noticing

Her diagnosis: the problem is not length — it's that spec docs are write-optimized, not read-optimized. She calls this "the curator's trap." The person writing the spec adds every detail that mattered to them; the person reading it needs only the details relevant to their task.

Write-optimized docs: useful at creation time. Read-optimized docs: useful at decision time. AI generates the first kind by default.

Pattern 09:15

The spec-as-contract

When teams treat an AI-generated doc as a binding source of truth, they stop questioning it. Chen calls this the spec-as-contract pattern — the document stops being a surface to think with and becomes a wall to stand behind.

Framework 14:40

Three modes of AI-assisted writing

Chen introduces a framework for thinking about where AI belongs in a documentation workflow. Most teams use only the first mode.

  • Generate — AI produces a first draft from a brief, meeting notes, or a prompt. Fast, often comprehensive, consistently missing the reader's perspective.
  • Distill — AI reorganizes and compresses existing content into a navigable structure. This is where most of the value is, and most teams skip it.
  • Operate — the document becomes a living reference surface that agents and humans navigate task-first, not sequentially.

Risk 26:30

The feedback loop problem

Chen argues that the solution is to eliminate the single-source spec entirely and replace it with a graph of smaller, linked artifacts — each owned by the team closest to it, each structured for scanning.

The contested claim

An audience member from a financial services firm pushed back hard: compliance requires single-document audit trails. Chen acknowledged this is a real constraint and said she should have qualified the claim — regulated industries cannot adopt this pattern without legal review.

Solution 32:10

What good looks like

Chen shows examples of scan-first documents: short, structured, heavily navigated. The reading time on these docs is under 3 minutes. Decision time — the time from opening the doc to making a choice — drops by roughly 60% in Foundry's internal measurement.

Scannable is a form of respect for the reader's time. It signals that you expect them to have other things to do.

Q&A 38:45

The hardest question

Best question from the floor: "How do you get teams to actually change their habits?" Chen's answer: you don't. You change the tool. If the output of your AI writing workflow is structurally required to be scannable — if the template enforces navigation, section headings, and progressive disclosure — teams produce better docs without changing their behavior.

Change the tool, not the habit. Structure the output to require good behavior.

Key Ideas

Pattern

The spec-as-contract trap

When teams stop re-reading a spec and start citing it as authoritative, it becomes a liability. The document no longer describes the system — it describes what people agreed to at one point in time.

Principle

Progressive disclosure as a writing principle

Structure documents so a reader can stop at the right depth. The person who needs the decision can stop at the summary. The person implementing can drill into the detail. Navigation is a feature, not decoration.

Framework

Generate, Distill, Operate

Separate the three phases. AI is strongest at Generate. The gap is at Distill — AI reorganization for read-optimization. Operate requires structure the first two phases rarely produce.

Takeaways

  • Run a "last read vs. last written" audit on your five most critical docs.
  • Separate Generate, Distill, and Operate phases in your AI writing workflow.
  • Add navigation and structure before sharing any doc over 20 lines.
  • Replace single-source specs with linked artifact graphs where compliance allows.
  • Change the tool, not the habit — structure the output to require good behavior.
